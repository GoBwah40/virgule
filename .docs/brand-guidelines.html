Brand guidelines · implemented version

Every idea, one shared decision.

Virgule helps a small group decide together: a birthday, a weekend, a project, an outing. Everyone suggests, everyone votes, and we keep the ideas that win the group over. The guidelines aim for a warm, readable app, designed for the phone you hold around the table.

  • Small groups, 6 people
  • Mobile first

Three principles

They settle things when a design decision is unclear.

Readable above all

Text at 16 px minimum, AA contrast everywhere, a single primary action per screen. Warm colors dress things up; they never replace a label.

Made for the thumb

44 px touch targets, important actions at the bottom of the screen, one column on mobile. Desktop gets the same interface, just roomier.

The group first

The app speaks to the group ("we"), shows who is there, and keeps votes anonymous. Nobody should feel judged for an idea.

Colors

An end-of-day-on-the-terrace palette: papaya, mango, sand. Olive green and pomegranate red are reserved for vote results, so they are never confused with the brand orange.

Dark theme

Each token has a dark value (marked "dark" in the palette): deep eggplant background, cream text, papaya and vote colors lightened to keep AA contrast. By default, the app follows the device setting.

Picker, in the footer
  • Three choices: Same as device (default), Light, Dark. The choice is remembered on the device.
  • Try it here: the whole page switches.
Exception: the QR code
  • The invite QR code stays ink on white, even in dark mode: a light QR code on a dark background scans poorly on many phones.
  • The white frame around it acts as the quiet zone for scanning.

The signature detail: a row of seats

A session hosts 6 people: a row of six seats around the table, from A to F. Each participant takes a seat: you see at a glance who is there and how many seats are left.

4 PARTICIPANTS OUT OF 6 · 2 FREE SEATS
  • Taken seat: first-name initial on pale mango, full first name on hover or long press.
  • You: solid mango background.
  • Free seat: dashed outline; tap it to copy the invite link.
  • The host: small crown above their seat ("Camille is hosting").
  • Aisle between C and D, seat letters in DM Mono: a discreet marker for counting, with no other decoration.

Tone and vocabulary

Friendly without being overfamiliar: we talk like the group's organized friend, the one who moves things forward without talking down. Short sentences, action verbs, no technical jargon.

"We" for the group, "you" for each person"Go for another round." "We" builds a sense of togetherness; when the app talks to one person, it addresses them directly and informally: "Suggest your ideas", "Tap to copy the link".
Warm, not chummyNo slang or emoji in the interface, at most one exclamation mark per screen. The enthusiasm comes from the colors, not the words.
Say what to do nextEvery error or waiting message says what is happening and what you can do. "This first name is already taken in the group. Add an initial."
First names rather than roles"Camille is preparing the topics" rather than "The facilitator is preparing the themes". We also avoid gendered wording: "the person hosting".

Decisions made

  1. Informal "you" (French tu) wherever "we" is not enough: "Your idea", "Suggest", "Tap…".
  2. Planning vocabulary: session, topics, ideas, round, recap; the app is for deciding anything, not a single kind of project.
  3. The row of seats is kept to show participants.
  4. The suggested topics are kept: Goal, Dates, Place, Budget, Priorities, Who does what.
